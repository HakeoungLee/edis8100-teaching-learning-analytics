# ✅ Final Submission Checklist

The last hour before you upload, spent well. Week 14, finals week, due at the date and time posted in Canvas.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **What you submit** | The complete Course Research Project as a Microsoft Word document, plus your AI interaction log in the Canvas "AI Reflection" submission |
| **Weight** | 30 percent of the course grade, scored out of 100 across eight criteria |
| **Minimum length** | 20 pages, excluding references and appendices |
| **When to start this checklist** | The day before you submit, not the hour before. Several items below take longer than they look. |
| **Late policy** | Unexcused late work forfeits 10 percent of the assignment score per day. If you need a different date, email the instructor **before** the deadline with a proposed one. |
| **Full handout** | `Week 14 Final Submission Checklist` on Canvas |

Work through the sections in order. The first section is the one that changes grades, and it is the one people skip because it is the hardest to do to your own writing.

## 1. Content completeness, against the rubric

The project is scored on eight criteria at 15, 12, 9, or 6 points each. Open [`README.md`](README.md) in this folder to the rubric table, and go criterion by criterion with your draft in the other window. For each one, find the page where a reader would see it. If you cannot point to a page, the criterion is not in the paper, no matter how clearly it is in your head.

- [ ] **Research problem and context.** The problem is stated in the first two pages, in a named setting, with specific people and a specific current state. A reader who stopped after page two could tell someone else what your study is about.
- [ ] **Conceptual framework and theory integration.** The framework is named, its constructs are defined, and at least one sentence connects a construct to a measurable trace. Trace that construct forward yourself: does it appear again in the methods section? If it vanishes after page 6, the integration criterion is where you will lose points.
- [ ] **Literature review and positioning.** The review synthesizes rather than summarizes. Test: does any paragraph discuss two or more studies together and say something about their relationship? If every paragraph is one study, you have an annotated bibliography in prose. The positioning claim, the gap or tension you are entering, appears as an explicit sentence.
- [ ] **Methodological plan.** Data sources, access, unit of analysis, cleaning and preparation, analysis methods, and visualization are all present and specific. Each research question maps to an analysis. A peer could tell you whether the plan is feasible, because there is enough there to judge.
- [ ] **Expected outcomes and contribution.** You have stated what you expect to find, specifically enough to be wrong, and what result would count as evidence against your framework.
- [ ] **Ethical, sociotechnical, and equity considerations.** This is a design section, not a paragraph of caveats near the end. Whose data, on what basis, which groups the measures could misrepresent, what the analysis makes invisible, and what happens to a person once a number is attached to them. Course readings are cited by name.
- [ ] **Actionability and relevance.** The decision, the decision maker, and the moment in their work where your evidence would arrive are all named. If the contribution is scholarly, the conversation it enters is identified specifically.
- [ ] **Writing quality and APA formatting.** See sections 3 and 4 below.
- [ ] **Every research question is answered by something.** Read your questions, then read your analysis plan. Any question with no matching analysis is either a question to cut or an analysis to add.
- [ ] **The peer review actually landed.** Reread your revision commitments from week 12. For each one, confirm you made the change, or be able to say why you did not. Reviewers notice when a draft comes back identical.

## 2. Formatting

- [ ] **Microsoft Word document** (`.docx`). Not a PDF, not a share link.
- [ ] **Double spaced** throughout, including the reference list.
- [ ] **11 or 12 point font**, one font, consistently.
- [ ] **1 inch margins** on all four sides.
- [ ] **Minimum 20 pages** of body text, excluding references and appendices. Check the count after the appendices are separated out, because it is easy to think you have cleared the line on the strength of a 40-item reference list.
- [ ] **Page numbers** present.
- [ ] **All tables and figures labeled and numbered**, each with a caption, and sources identified where the material is not yours.
- [ ] **Every table and figure is referred to in the text** before it appears. A figure nobody points at is decoration.
- [ ] **Headings follow APA 7 levels** and are used consistently, so that the structure is visible from the headings alone.
- [ ] **Your name and a real title** are on it. A working title with brackets still in it has been submitted more than once.

## 3. Appendices

Appendices do not count toward the 20-page minimum.

- [ ] **Appendix: Code.** If you wrote any, include it. Notebooks from this course count. Include the code you actually ran, as text rather than as a screenshot, with enough comments that a reader can follow the sequence. If you adapted a course notebook, say which one.
- [ ] **Appendix: additional figures and tables**, if you have material that supports the argument but would interrupt it.
- [ ] Each appendix is **labeled and referred to from the body text** at the point where it becomes relevant.
- [ ] **Your AI interactions go to Canvas, not here.** The full record, per the course AI policy, is pasted into a Word file and attached to the Canvas "AI Reflection" submission, which is a separate upload from the paper itself. The syllabus also allows the full exchange to be carried as an appendix in the paper; a copy here is welcome but does not replace the Canvas submission, which is required either way.
- [ ] Your **reflection answers go in the Canvas text box** on that submission page, not in the Word file and not in the paper: the four questions from the syllabus, copied in and answered. You are not graded on how much AI you used. You are graded on the work, and on having told the truth about the process.

## 4. APA 7 mechanics

The Purdue OWL settles most of these faster than a search engine will.

- [ ] **Every in-text citation appears in the reference list.** Check them one at a time. This is tedious and it is the single most common formatting deduction.
- [ ] **Every reference-list entry is cited somewhere in the text.** Sources you read but did not use come out.
- [ ] **Author and year formats are right**: parenthetical versus narrative, `&` inside parentheses and "and" in running text, `et al.` from the first citation for three or more authors.
- [ ] **The reference list is alphabetical**, hanging indented, double spaced.
- [ ] **DOIs are formatted as `https://doi.org/...`** and are present wherever the source has one.
- [ ] **Journal titles and volume numbers are italicized**, article titles are in sentence case.
- [ ] **Direct quotations carry page or paragraph numbers.** If you cannot find the page number, paraphrase instead.
- [ ] **Numbers, abbreviations, and bias-free language** follow APA 7. The bias-free language guidance is not decorative in a paper about equity in measurement.

## 5. The read-aloud pass

Do this last, out loud, at full volume, in one sitting. It will take about an hour for 20 pages. Nothing else finds these problems.

- [ ] **Read the whole paper aloud.** Every sentence you stumble over gets rewritten, without exception and without arguing with yourself about why the stumble was your fault rather than the sentence's.
- [ ] **Listen for the paragraph that has no job.** If you cannot say what a paragraph is doing after you have just read it aloud, cut it or give it a purpose.
- [ ] **Listen for repetition.** Proposals that grew by accretion say the same thing in the introduction, the framework, and the methods. Say it once, in the strongest place.
- [ ] **Check your transitions between major sections.** A reader arriving at the methods section should already know why these methods.
- [ ] **Read the first and last paragraph back to back.** They should be about the same study, and the last one should have earned something.
- [ ] **Spell check, then read for the words spell check approves of**: form and from, its and it's, effect and affect, and every person's name you cited.
- [ ] **Open the final `.docx` fresh** and scroll through it end to end. Look for broken tables, orphaned headings, figures that moved, and comment bubbles you meant to delete.
- [ ] **Confirm the file name** is something a human can identify: your last name, the course, and the assignment.

## Before you close the laptop

- [ ] The paper is uploaded to Canvas.
- [ ] The AI interaction log is uploaded to the Canvas "AI Reflection" submission.
- [ ] You have a copy saved somewhere that is not only your laptop.
- [ ] You noted the one thing you would do next if this became a real study. In February, when you are deciding whether to keep going, that note will be worth more than the grade.
